## 1. Schwartz's theory of basic human values

**Original theory.** Schwartz (1992) [web] defined values as trans-situational goals that act as guiding principles in a person's life. He identified 10 motivationally distinct values: self-direction, stimulation, hedonism, achievement, power, security, conformity, tradition, benevolence and universalism. He tested them in 20 countries. The values form a **circular motivational continuum**: neighbouring values can be pursued in the same act (hedonism and stimulation, for example), while opposite values conflict (stimulation and security). Compatibility falls off with distance around the circle (Schwartz & Cieciuch, 2022 [PM]).

**Organizing principles** (Schwartz et al., 2012 [PM]; Schwartz & Cieciuch, 2022 [PM]):

- **Two bipolar dimensions.** *Openness to change* (self-direction, stimulation, part of hedonism) is opposed to *conservation* (security, conformity, tradition). *Self-enhancement* (achievement, power) is opposed to *self-transcendence* (benevolence, universalism).
- **Personal vs social focus.** Openness and self-enhancement values concern outcomes for oneself. Conservation and self-transcendence values concern outcomes for others or for institutions.
- **Growth (anxiety-free) vs self-protection (anxiety-avoidance).** Openness and self-transcendence values express self-expansion. Conservation and self-enhancement values express avoiding threat and controlling anxiety. The growth/protection split is clearer in more developed societies: the Human Development Index correlated .48 with structural fit on that dimension across 49 groups (Schwartz & Cieciuch, 2022 [PM]).

**Refined theory: 19 values** (Schwartz et al., 2012, *JPSP* 103, 663–688 [PM]). The definitions below are quoted from Table 1 of Schwartz & Cieciuch (2022) [PM, full text], which reproduces the 2012 and 2017 definitions.

| Higher-order value | Value (code) | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Openness to change | Self-direction–thought (SDT) | Freedom to cultivate one's own ideas and abilities |
| | Self-direction–action (SDA) | Freedom to determine one's own actions |
| | Stimulation (ST) | Excitement, novelty and challenge in life |
| Openness / Self-enhancement border | Hedonism (HE) | Pleasure and sensuous gratification for oneself |
| Self-enhancement | Achievement (AC) | Personal success through demonstrating competence according to social standards |
| | Power–dominance (POD) | Power through exercising control over people |
| | Power–resources (POR) | Power through control of material and social resources |
| Self-enhancement / Conservation border | Face (FAC) | Security and power through maintaining one's public image and avoiding humiliation |
| Conservation | Security–personal (SEP) | Safety in one's immediate environment |
| | Security–societal (SES) | Safety and stability in the wider society |
| | Tradition (TR) | Maintaining and preserving cultural, family or religious traditions |
| | Conformity–rules (COR) | Compliance with rules, laws and formal obligations |
| | Conformity–interpersonal (COI) | Avoidance of upsetting or harming other people |
| Conservation / Self-transcendence border | Humility (HUM) | Recognizing one's insignificance in the larger scheme of things |
| Self-transcendence | Universalism–nature (UNN) | Preservation of the natural environment |
| | Universalism–concern (UNC) | Commitment to equality, justice and protection for all people |
| | Universalism–tolerance (UNT) | Acceptance and understanding of those who are different from oneself |
| | Benevolence–caring (BEC) | Devotion to the welfare of in-group members |
| | Benevolence–dependability (BED) | Being a reliable and trustworthy member of the in-group |

A precursor study in Poland (Cieciuch & Schwartz, 2012 [PM]) separated 15 values, including two subtypes of achievement: *ambition* and *showing success*.

**Cross-cultural evidence and the PVQ-RR.** The PVQ-RR has 57 portrait items ("It is important to him/her to…"). In 49 cultural groups (N = 53,472; 32 languages) it reliably measured 15 of the 19 values in most groups and two more in many. It was metrically invariant for almost all values, and multidimensional scaling reproduced the theorized circular order (Schwartz & Cieciuch, 2022 [PM]). The pan-cultural importance hierarchy is stable: **benevolence, self-direction and universalism rank highest, and tradition and power rank lowest** (same source). Sagiv & Schwartz (2022) [PM] review the cross-cultural evidence.

## 2. Values and wellbeing

- **Sagiv & Schwartz (2000)** [web; DOI not resolved] tested two pathways.
  - *Direct relations* (N = 1,261; Israel, East and West Germany): achievement, self-direction and stimulation related to *affective* wellbeing as predicted, which was positively, while tradition, conformity and security related negatively. None related to *cognitive* wellbeing (life satisfaction). [Signs: snippet]
  - *Congruity* (business vs psychology students): wellbeing depended on whether a person's values matched the values that dominate their environment.
- **"Healthy" values.** Bobowik et al. (2011) [web] used ESS and Basque samples, including immigrants. Hedonic and psychological wellbeing went with openness and individualist values, and went negatively with power and conservation values. The authors read this as a near-universal link between "healthy values" and wellbeing. Sagiv, Roccas & Oppenheim-Weller (2015) [web; pp. 103–120, no DOI found] organize the field into three pathways: *healthy values*, *valued-goal attainment*, and *person–environment congruence*. The 2004 first-edition chapter by Sagiv, Roccas and Hazan [web, author order unverified] used the same three pathways.
- **Culture moderates.** Sortheix & Schwartz (2017) [web] used representative samples from 32 countries (N = 121,495). Openness values went with higher life satisfaction and less depressive affect, and conservation values with lower. The effects were small and varied with national context. Schwartz & Sortheix (2018) [web; page range differs by source] review the work and describe growth values as conducive to SWB and self-protection values as undermining it [snippet].
- **Direction of effect.** A registered-report panel study found bidirectional cross-lagged effects between openness values and SWB (Grosz, Schwartz & Lechner, 2021 [web; catalogues disagree on author order]).
- **No meta-analysis** of Schwartz values and SWB turned up in my searches [unverified absence]. The correlations reported are mostly small (|r| ≈ .05–.20).

**Intrinsic vs extrinsic aspirations.**
- Kasser & Ryan (1993) [PM]: when financial success is relatively central among a person's aspirations, it goes with lower wellbeing and adjustment.
- Kasser & Ryan (1996) [web]: *extrinsic* aspirations (financial success, social recognition, appealing appearance) went with lower vitality and self-actualization and more physical symptoms. *Intrinsic* aspirations (self-acceptance and growth, affiliation, community feeling) went with higher wellbeing.
- **Dittmar, Bond, Hurst & Kasser (2014)** [PM] meta-analysed 753 effects from 259 samples. Materialism related to lower wellbeing (materialist values and beliefs r = −.19, ρ = −.24; relative importance of materialist goals r = −.16). The link was stronger in materialism-supporting contexts, and **poor need satisfaction** mediated it.

## 3. Wellbeing frameworks

- **SDT basic needs** (Ryan & Deci, 2000 [PM]; 2017 [web]): **autonomy, competence, relatedness**. When these are satisfied, self-motivation and mental health improve.
- **Beneficence.** Martela & Ryan (2016) [PM; *J Pers* 84(6), online 2015] found that a sense of beneficence (being able to give) predicts wellbeing independently of the three needs.
- **Hedonic vs eudaimonic.** Ryan & Deci (2001) [PM]: the hedonic tradition defines wellbeing as pleasure attained and pain avoided; the eudaimonic tradition defines it as meaning and full functioning. Huta & Waterman (2014) [web] classify definitions as *orientations* (values, motives), *behaviours*, *experiences* or *functioning*. ACT values sit closest to eudaimonic orientations and behaviours.
- **Ryff (1989)** [web], six dimensions: self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, personal growth.
- **PERMA** (Seligman, 2011 [web]): Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment.
- **VanderWeele (2017)** [PM]: five flourishing domains (happiness and life satisfaction; mental and physical health; meaning and purpose; character and virtue; close social relationships). A sixth domain, *financial and material stability*, is treated as "enabling", and the 12-item "secure flourishing" index includes it [snippet for the sixth domain].
- **Meaning in life** (Martela & Steger, 2016 [web]): *coherence* (life makes sense), *purpose* (core goals and direction), *significance* (life has inherent value and is worth living).

## 4. Character strengths (VIA)

Peterson & Seligman (2004) [web] list **24 strengths under 6 virtues** [list: snippet plus VIA Institute records; the virtue assignment was theoretical, and factor analyses do not recover six factors]:

| Virtue | Strengths |
|---|---|
| Wisdom & knowledge | creativity, curiosity, judgment (open-mindedness), love of learning, perspective |
| Courage | bravery, perseverance, honesty (authenticity, integrity), zest (vitality) |
| Humanity | love, kindness, social intelligence |
| Justice | teamwork, fairness, leadership |
| Temperance | forgiveness, humility, prudence, self-regulation |
| Transcendence | appreciation of beauty & excellence, gratitude, hope, humor, spirituality |

Park, Peterson & Seligman (2004) [web; N = 5,299] studied strengths and life satisfaction. **Hope, zest, gratitude, love and curiosity** had the strongest associations [snippet: Peterson et al., 2007 restate this five-strength finding].

## 5. Moral psychology

- **Moral Foundations Theory.**
  - Graham et al. (2011 [PM]; 2013 [web; DOI partially verified]) give five foundations: care/harm, fairness/cheating, loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, sanctity/degradation. Liberty/oppression is discussed as a candidate sixth [unverified in the 2013 text].
  - Atari et al. (2023) [PM; 25 populations, MFQ-2] showed that **equality and proportionality are distinct foundations**, giving six: care, equality, proportionality, loyalty, authority, purity.
- **Morality-as-Cooperation** (Curry, Mullins & Whitehouse, 2019 [web]; 60 societies). Seven cooperative behaviours: **helping kin, helping your group, reciprocating, being brave, deferring to superiors, dividing disputed resources, respecting prior possession**. All were rated positively wherever observed.

## 6. ACT domains

These are already covered in RESEARCH.md. **VLQ** (Wilson et al., 2010), 10 domains: family (other than couples and parenting), couples/intimate relations, parenting, friends/social life, work, education/training, recreation/fun, spirituality, citizenship/community, physical self-care. **Bull's-Eye** (Lundgren et al., 2012), 4 domains: work/education, leisure, relationships, personal growth/health.

## 7. Lexical and text studies of values vocabularies

- **Renner (2003)** [web] started from a German lexicon and took 383 value nouns and 299 adjectives, rated by 456 people. Five factors emerged. Reported labels differ between the noun and adjective solutions and across reports: Balance/Harmony, Intellectualism, Conservatism, Religiosity/Salvation, Profit/Power [snippet].
- **Aavik & Allik (2002)** [web] took 560 Estonian value words and found six components that only partly matched the Schwartz Value Survey. A "natural" values lexicon contains content (e.g., health, intellectual, religious clusters) that Schwartz's theory-driven sampling under-weights.
- **Ponizovskiy et al. (2020)** [web] built the **Personal Values Dictionary** (words mapped to the 10 values) and tested it on text from more than 180,000 people. Inter-value correlations reproduced the circumplex, but correlations with self-reported values were only r ≈ .1–.4, for 7 of 10 values.
- **Boyd et al. (2015)** [web] derived value themes bottom-up from free text (767 MTurk participants; 130,828 Facebook users). Language-based value measures predicted behaviour better than questionnaires.
- **Wilson, Shen & Mihalcea (2018)** [web] released a hierarchical lexicon of 50 value categories.
- **Cieciuch & Schwartz (2012)** [PM] showed the circle can be partitioned more finely than 10 values.

**Implications.** (i) A value word maps onto a motivational region only loosely, so the card *descriptions*, not the names, should carry the mapping. (ii) The circle is an empirical result of co-ratings. Lodestar could rebuild its own map by multidimensional scaling of anonymised pile co-occurrences, which is how Schwartz derived his. (iii) Lexical studies flag health, intellect and religiosity as salient clusters, and the deck covers all three.

| Framework | Absent | Thin | Over-represented |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schwartz-19 | POD, POR, FAC (deliberate) | AC (no ambition/excellence card), SES, COR (only Self-discipline/Order), COI (no politeness/courtesy), UNC's *equality* (implicit only) | BEC (16 cards), BED (8) |
| VIA 24 | **hope**, **zest** | social intelligence (Empathy only), prudence (no card) | kindness, love, honesty (three cards each) |
| SDT | none | competence is fine; autonomy fine | relatedness |
| PERMA / VanderWeele | *Meaning/purpose* as a card; *Accomplishment*; financial/material stability; *Engagement* (absorption, flow) | Positive emotion (Fun, Pleasure, Gratitude) | Relationships, character |
| Ryff / Martela & Steger | *Purpose* | environmental mastery (Order, Responsibility), coherence (Wisdom, Self-awareness) | positive relations |
| MFT / MaC | authority/deference, purity/sanctity, reciprocity, property | equality (Justice) | care |
| VLQ | none | **work** (no work-specific quality such as craft, service or professionalism), parenting (only Nurturing), citizenship | family/couples/friends |

Two gaps stand out. **Hope** and **zest** are two of the five strengths most associated with life satisfaction (Park et al., 2004), and the deck has neither. **Purpose** is a component of Ryff, PERMA, VanderWeele and Martela & Steger, and the deck has no card for it either. The skew toward care and the growth half also means that people whose priorities are conservation or self-enhancement (security, duty, achievement, respect for elders) will see fewer cards that describe them. The binding moral foundations (authority, purity) and Schwartz's conformity values are where this is most visible.

**Candidate additions**, each phrased as a quality of action:
- *Hope*: acting with hope and keeping a future worth working toward in view.
- *Vitality/Zest*: approaching life with energy and wholeheartedness. Avoid wording it as a feeling.
- *Purpose*: living with a sense of direction.
- *Excellence/Achievement*: striving to do things to a high standard. This avoids AC's "showing success" subtype.
- *Equality*.
- *Courtesy*.
- *Duty* or *Respect for elders/heritage* (optional; see pliance caveat in (d)).
- *Providing/Stewardship*: looking after resources so the people I care about are secure. This is the action-quality form of material stability.
- *Engagement/Absorption*.

## (c) Merge and split candidates

**Merges.** Each pair or group below shares a Schwartz-19 value, a VIA strength and near-synonymous descriptions.
1. **Persistence and Diligence** (AC; perseverance). Both say "seeing things through". Merge, or rewrite Diligence as care and quality in work.
2. **Caring, Kindness, Nurturing, Encouragement** (BEC; kindness). Keep *Kindness* and *Nurturing* (the parenting/mentoring role). Fold *Encouragement* into Nurturing and *Caring* into Kindness. Keep **Compassion** separate, because it is defined by a response to suffering.
3. **Love and Connection** (BEC; love). *Connection* ("fully present and engaged") is really Love plus Mindfulness. Merge it into Love, or rename it *Presence*.
4. **Growth and Learning** (SDT; love of learning). *Growth* is the most outcome-like card ("evolve as a person"). Keep it only if it is distinguished as personal development, as in Kasser's intrinsic "self-acceptance and growth".
5. **Honesty, Integrity, Authenticity.** VIA folds all three into one strength. They are defensible as three cards because they point at others, at principle and at the self. Watch that Integrity ("in line with my principles") doesn't duplicate the others.
6. **Reliability and Trust** (BED). *Trust* contains "being trustworthy", which duplicates Reliability. Keep only the "extending trust" half.

**Splits.** These cards are double-barrelled across regions of the circle.
1. **Freedom**: own choices (SDA) plus respecting others' choices (UNT). Trim it to the first, and let Respect or Open-mindedness carry the second.
2. **Safety**: protecting myself (SEP) plus creating security for others or society (SES).
3. **Peace**: inner calm (closest to Mindfulness or Acceptance) plus harmony with others (COI/UNC).
4. **Acceptance**: covers self, others and life. Self-acceptance overlaps Self-compassion and Ryff's self-acceptance.
5. **Humility**: the deck's wording is VIA modesty plus intellectual humility ("open to being wrong"), which differs from Schwartz HUM ("one's insignificance"). Choose one. Intellectual humility already overlaps Open-mindedness.
6. **Spirituality**: religious practice and heritage (TR) vs transcendent connection (UNN/HUM; VIA spirituality). It may need two cards for religious users.
7. **Leadership**: guiding others (AC/POD) vs serving or inspiring (BEC).

**Noun-domain cards.** *Family, Community, Nature, Health, Tradition* and *Spirituality* name domains or objects rather than qualities. Their descriptions rescue them, but ACT authors treat domains (the VLQ) and values (qualities within domains) as separate layers. Consider showing these as domain tags rather than cards.

## (d) Caution: ACT values vs Schwartz values

**Schwartz values are desirable end-states.** Schwartz defines a value as a "transsituational goal" and "a belief about the importance of desirable goals" (Schwartz & Cieciuch, 2022, quoting Schwartz 1992). Several of the 19 are **outcomes or states** a person can possess:
- power–resources (wealth)
- power–dominance (control)
- face (image)
- achievement's "showing success"
- the security values

**ACT values are qualities of action.** ACT defines values as freely chosen qualities of ongoing action whose reinforcement lies in the doing (Wilson & DuFrene, 2009; Plumb et al., 2009 [web]; Harris, 2019). Under that definition, "wealth" or "being respected" is a goal or a hoped-for outcome. The wellbeing literature points the same way. Extrinsic aspirations are linked to lower wellbeing through thwarted need satisfaction (Kasser & Ryan, 1996; Dittmar et al., 2014). Face and power are the values most driven by social contingencies, which is where *pliance* operates (see RESEARCH.md).

**How ACT practitioners handle outcome-type choices** [practice heuristics, paraphrased; not verified verbatim]:
1. **Treat the item as a goal and ladder up.** Ask "If you had that, what would it let you do or be? How would you treat people?" The answer is usually Providing, Freedom, Contribution, Mastery or Security for loved ones (Harris; LeJeune & Luoma, 2019 [web]).
2. **Do not argue about it.** Values are chosen, not justified (Wilson & Murrell, 2004). Explore the *function* of the item: approach or avoidance, vitality or obligation.
3. **Screen for pliance.** Ask whether the item would survive if no one would ever know.

**Design implications.**
- Keep Wealth, Power, Status and Fame out of the deck. Lodestar already omits them.
- When a user writes such a value on a custom card, show a gentle "what would that give you?" prompt instead of rejecting it.
- If you add Achievement or Providing, phrase them as qualities: "striving for excellence", "providing for those I care about".
- **Do not use the wellbeing findings to steer users toward "healthy" values.** The effects are small, vary by culture and depend on the environment (Sagiv & Schwartz, 2000; Sortheix & Schwartz, 2017). Steering would also invite pliance. Use this literature for **coverage and map structure only**. A balanced deck lets a conservation-oriented user find cards that describe them honestly.
